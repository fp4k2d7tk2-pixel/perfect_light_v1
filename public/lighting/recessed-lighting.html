Recessed Lighting

Recessed Lighting Installation in Chicago

Recessed lighting, done well, disappears into the ceiling and quietly changes how a room feels. Done poorly, it looks like a runway. Perfect Light plans the layout first — then installs cleanly, tests the dimming, and finishes with a properly patched ceiling.

The Perfect Light Approach

Layout first. Holes second.

Most complaints about recessed lighting come from decisions made before the drill ever touched the ceiling. Fixture spacing, beam angle, brightness, color temperature, and dimming compatibility all get sorted out on paper before any drywall is opened.

What Gets Considered

The details behind a good layout.

Room use

How the space is actually lived in — kitchen prep, reading, entertaining, watching a screen.

Fixture spacing

Enough coverage for even light, without turning the ceiling into a grid of dots.

Color temperature

Warm 2700K for living spaces, brighter 3000K or 3500K where task light is needed.

Dimming compatibility

Matched dimmer and LED driver so the light dims smoothly and doesn't flicker.

Ceiling condition

Plaster, drywall, joist location, insulation, and existing wiring all factor in.

Beam angle

Narrow for accent, wide for general ambient. Chosen per zone, not per house.

Trim & aperture

Smaller apertures for a cleaner look; trim finish matched to the ceiling.

Controls

Zoning, dimming, and optional smart controls planned before rough-in.

Common Rooms

Where recessed lighting earns its keep.

Kitchens, living rooms, primary bedrooms, hallways, and finished basements are the most common. Bathrooms, home offices, closets, and stair landings often benefit as well — when the layout is right for that specific space.

Recessed lighting rarely does the whole job on its own. In most rooms it works best paired with pendants, a chandelier, sconces, or under-cabinet lighting to build proper layers.

A few honest notes:

  • Older Chicago homes with plaster ceilings and blown-in insulation take more time. That gets flagged before work begins, not after.
  • Drywall or plaster patching is not automatically included. Whether Perfect Light handles it or a finisher does is confirmed in writing up front.
  • "More cans" is not always better. Sometimes the answer is fewer, better-placed lights on a good dimmer.
