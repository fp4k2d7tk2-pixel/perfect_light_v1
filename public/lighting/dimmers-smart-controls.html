Dimmers & Smart Controls

Dimmer & Smart Lighting Control Installation

Great lighting is not just about the fixture — it's about the control. The right dimmer or smart switch is what turns "the lights are on" into "this room feels good." Perfect Light matches dimmers to LEDs, replaces flickering setups, and installs whole-home lighting scenes that actually stay set.

Why Dimming Matters

The switch is half the lighting.

Most homes have great fixtures paired with mediocre switches. A quality dimmer transforms a bright, flat room into something warm and adjustable — the same fixture at 100% and at 30% is essentially two different rooms.

When LEDs flicker, buzz, or refuse to dim to a low level, it's almost always a dimmer/LED compatibility problem. That's a solvable problem, not a broken fixture.

What Perfect Light Installs

Controls that actually match how you live.

LED-compatible dimmers

Matched to the fixture and driver so the light dims smoothly to a warm low, without flicker or buzz.

3-way and multi-location dimming

Long hallways, stairwells, and open rooms controlled cleanly from every entrance.

Smart switches

Lutron Caseta, Lutron RA, and comparable systems — installed and configured, not just wired in.

Occupancy & vacancy sensors

Closets, laundry rooms, mudrooms, and other spaces where "lights left on" is a recurring problem.

Lighting scenes

One button for "everyday," one for "dinner," one for "off" — consistent across the room.

Timers & schedules

Exterior lights, accent lights, and holiday circuits on schedules that follow the sun.

Whole-home upgrades

Coordinated dimmer families, matched finishes, and consistent behavior on every wall.

Retrofit & troubleshooting

Diagnose flickering, buzzing, or dead-zone dimming and replace with the right control.

Smart, Not Complicated

Smart controls should work when the internet doesn't.

Smart lighting is only useful when it's reliable. Perfect Light focuses on systems that use physical wall switches as the primary control, with app and voice access as a bonus — not as the only way to turn the light on.

The goal is a home that anyone can walk into and use, whether or not they know it has smart controls behind the walls.

Before recommending a system:

  • How much of the home is being covered.
  • Whether existing fixtures will stay or change.
  • What "everyday" and "guest" use actually looks like.
  • Any existing smart-home ecosystem already in place.
  • How much control you actually want — more is not always better.
